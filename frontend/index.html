Our mission is simple: turn destructive urban runoff back into water a city can use — by telling every neighborhood exactly where it needs to build for it, using nothing but free satellite data.

The Problem

Indian cities are flooding more, with less warning

Rapid, unplanned urbanization has paved over the lakes and wetlands that once absorbed monsoon rainfall. The result is frequent, severe flooding — and no existing system tells a city exactly which street needs which fix.

The Solution

Why sponge cities should be built now

A "sponge city" absorbs rainwater where it falls — through rain gardens, permeable pavement, and groundwater recharge — instead of letting it run off into overwhelmed drains. Cities like Chennai, Mumbai, and Kochi are already planning this. Bengaluru isn't yet.

Absorb, don't discharge

Rain gardens and permeable surfaces let water soak in locally, reducing runoff at the source instead of overloading drains downstream.

Recharge groundwater

Recharge wells replenish the water table — turning a flood risk into a long-term water resource for the city.

Near-zero deployment cost

Built entirely on free satellite and government data — any ward can replicate this without new hardware or sensors.

About This Tool

Methodology, data sources, and honest limitations.

What this is

Jalamrit identifies which 100m-by-100m patches of a neighborhood are genuinely prone to flooding — and recommends a specific fix (rain garden, permeable pavement, or groundwater recharge well) — using only free satellite data. No sensors, no hardware.

How risk is calculated

  • Elevation, slope, and imperviousness pulled from Google Earth Engine (SRTM, ESA WorldCover)
  • Distance to the nearest mapped stormwater drain (real BBMP/KSRSAC drain network, 6,848 segments)
  • Flood detection via Sentinel-1 radar, cross-checked against a dry-season control to remove permanent lakes from the results
  • A Random Forest model trained on confirmed historical flood events, cross-validated

Validated against

  • Official BBMP zone-wise flood vulnerability lists
  • National Water Data Portal government rainfall station records, 1900–2025
  • A peer-reviewed 2026 academic study on Bellandur Ward flood risk (ETASR journal)
Honest limitations: Model accuracy ranges 0.55–0.78 ROC-AUC across tested zones — meaningfully better than guessing, strongest in Bellandur. This is a static risk map, not a real-time forecast: we identify where infrastructure is needed, not when the next flood will hit. We tested Karnataka's public hourly rainfall telemetry for real-time forecasting and found genuine coverage gaps, which we report rather than paper over.

Citizen reporting

Reports submitted through this tool are stored and visible to everyone using it, including the Government Portal view. This is a working demo — in a real deployment, reports would route directly to the relevant ward office's system.
